title: Hierarchies
description: The Hierarchy panel shows the callers and callees of a function, and the base and derived types of a type, as trees you open level by level.
---

The **Hierarchy** panel shows who calls a function and what it calls, or which types a type derives from and which derive from it. Each
level of the tree loads when you open it, so you can follow calls and types as far as you need.

## Show a call hierarchy

<Steps>

1. Put the caret on a function or method and press <Keys>Ctrl+Alt+H</Keys>, or right-click it and choose **Show Call Hierarchy**.
2. The **Hierarchy** panel opens with the function at the top and its callers below. Open a caller to see its own callers.
3. Click **Callees** to see the functions it calls instead, and **Callers** to go back.

</Steps>

Each row shows the function's name, its detail, such as the type it belongs to, and its file and line. A caller that calls more than once
shows how many calls. Selecting a row with the keyboard shows the call in the editor; <Keys>Enter</Keys> or a click goes there.

## Show a type hierarchy

Put the caret on a type and press <Keys>Ctrl+Alt+Shift+H</Keys>, or right-click it and choose **Show Type Hierarchy**. The panel lists the
types that derive from it or implement it. Click **Supertypes** to see the types it derives from and the interfaces it implements, and
**Subtypes** to go back.

## Where hierarchies come from

Hierarchies come from the language's server and from plugins. When the language has none for the caret's place, a short message beside the
caret says so. The [Editor extensions](/plugins/language-features#call-and-type-hierarchies) page shows how a plugin adds them.
